\begin{abstract}
We present the complete engineering specification of the \shbt{} (Synthetic-dimension Holographic
Boundary Tensor, Reconfigurable) quantum computer and of the software stack that qualifies it. The
machine realises an effective two-dimensional conformal boundary theory on a discrete photonic
substrate whose operational state space is fixed by the dimension vector $(k_l,k_q,K)=(26,8,312)$:
$k_l=26$ is the embedding dimension of the classical control boundary, $k_q=8=2^3$ is the local
Hilbert-space dimension of each three-qubit hexamer sub-cluster, and $K=312=52\times 6$ is the number
of active InP/InGaAsP microcavity Voronoi channels. Entanglement is seeded by a
$72\unit{GHz}$ InP/InGaAs Dynamical Casimir Effect (DCE) substrate that emits two-mode squeezed vacuum
at $36\unit{GHz}$ under a pump density of $895.0\unit{W\,cm^{-2}}$. Phonons are evacuated through a
single-crystal sapphire heat-capacity reservoir ($V\ge 1911.0\unit{cm^3}$, $Z=44.178\unit{MRayl}$)
impedance-matched to the helium bath by a nanoporous silica aerogel quarter-wave layer
($d_m=6.395\unit{nm}$, $Z_m=1.1512\unit{MRayl}$) at $4.2\unit{K}$. The boundary code is an
approximate holographic error-correcting code: the HKLL reconstruction map obeys the operator-norm
bound $\lVert W^\dagger W-P_\code\rVert_\op\le 3.367\times10^{-3}$ and the excitation algebra is
trace-free, $\Tr(\hat O_{\mathrm{exc}})=0$, which forces the electric part of the bulk Weyl tensor to
vanish, $E_{\mu\nu}=0$. Control electronics operate against the Landauer erasure limit
$k_BT\ln 2=4.0194\times10^{-23}\unit{J\,bit^{-1}}$ at $4.2\unit{K}$, and logical gates are compiled by a
depth-9 Solovay--Kitaev (SK-9) topological compiler over a 124-word braid generating set with error
floor $\epsilon_9\le10^{-230}$. The bare-metal \shbtos{} microkernel executes from a zero-heap
2112-byte \texttt{UnifiedStinespringFrame} arena split into a 640-byte visible register
($\eta_A=10/33$) and a 1472-byte dark ledger ($\eta_D=23/33$), runs a $1.14\unit{ns}$ AVX-512 telemetry loop
that tracks eigenvector rigidity $\delta_\Phi<10^{-12}$, and arms a $2.50\unit{ns}$ electro-optic bias
shunt. We close with the digital-twin simulation engine---cryogenic multiphysics, Monte Carlo statistical
process control, a matrix-product-state hardware-in-the-loop testbench and a Z3 formal proof of the
$105.90\unit{ns}$ post-quench recovery bound---and show how it discharges the 35-item verification and
validation matrix V-01 through V-35.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fault-tolerant quantum computation has historically been pursued by scaling the number of physical
qubits until the surface-code threshold is comfortably exceeded. The \shbt{} architecture takes a
different route. Rather than treating the physical layer as an array of weakly coupled two-level
systems, it treats the layer as the discrete \emph{boundary} of an effective holographic bulk. Logical
information is stored in the bulk degrees of freedom that are reconstructed from the boundary by a
smearing kernel, and the redundancy of that reconstruction is what furnishes error correction.
The paradigm shift is therefore threefold:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Geometry replaces counting.} The boundary is a two-torus $\mathbb T^2$ discretised into
  $K=312$ Voronoi cells with UV cutoff $k_c=\pi/a$, $a=12.51\unit{\mu m}$. The code distance is a
  property of the bulk depth, not of a qubit count.
  \item \textbf{Photons replace superconducting circuits.} Every boundary cell is an InP/InGaAsP
  high-$Q$ microcavity. Six cavities form a $C_6$-symmetric hexamer and 52 hexamers tile the wafer.
  The third qubit direction of each hexamer is \emph{synthetic}: it is a frequency ladder generated by
  electro-optic modulation at $\Omega_{\mathrm{EOM}}=2\pi\times14.50\unit{GHz}$.
  \item \textbf{A microkernel replaces firmware.} All real-time behaviour---quench recovery, isometry
  remapping, telemetry, and error-correction decoding---is executed by a freestanding C11 microkernel,
  \shbtos{}, whose timing bounds are proven with an SMT solver rather than merely measured.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The canonical branch $(k_l,k_q,K)=(26,8,312)$}

The state space of the machine is fixed by three integers.

\begin{definition}[Dimension vector]
The \emph{dimension vector} of an \shbt{} instance is $(k_l,k_q,K)$ where
$k_l$ is the real dimension of the classical control manifold in which the boundary EFT is embedded,
$k_q=\dim\Hil_{\mathrm{node}}$ is the local Hilbert-space dimension per Voronoi node, and $K$ is the
number of active nodes. The total physical Hilbert space is
$\Hil_{\mathrm{phys}}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{K}\Hil_{\mathrm{node}}\cong(\mathbb C^{k_q})^{\otimes K}$.
\end{definition}

On the canonical branch $k_q=8=2^3$: each node carries three qubits---two spatial polarisation/mode
qubits of the microcavity pair and one synthetic-frequency qubit. With $K=312$ the physical space has
dimension $8^{312}=2^{936}$. The classical control boundary is embedded in $k_l=26$ dimensions: the
$52$ hexamer centres, each carrying a two-dimensional control coordinate (bias phase and drive
amplitude), are projected onto a $26$-dimensional manifold obtained by identifying the two
$C_6$-conjugate halves of each hexamer pair, $52\times2/4=26$. This is the same $26$ that appears as
the critical dimension of the bosonic worldsheet theory, and it is not a coincidence: the boundary
EFT is a $c_{\mathrm{eff}}=24.00$ theory (requirement V-01) plus the two light-cone directions, and
$k_l=c_{\mathrm{eff}}+2$ is the smallest embedding in which the modular anomaly vanishes to the
required $\Delta Z/Z\le1.2\times10^{-7}$ (V-03).

\subsection{Dynamical Casimir seeding of two-mode squeezed vacuum}
\label{sec:dce}

Entanglement in the \shbt{} is not created by gates acting on a product state; it is emitted by the
substrate. The InP/InGaAs heteroepitaxial layer beneath each hexamer is a mirror whose effective
position is modulated by a $72\unit{GHz}$ electro-absorptive drive. A mirror moving with
$x(t)=x_0\sin\Omega t$ in a cavity of frequency $\omega_0$ converts vacuum fluctuations into photon
pairs whenever $\Omega=\omega_a+\omega_b$. For the degenerate branch $\omega_a=\omega_b=\Omega/2$ the
emitted pairs sit at $36\unit{GHz}$, and the emitted state is the two-mode squeezed vacuum
$$
\lvert\psi_{\mathrm{TMSV}}\rangle
=\hat S_{ab}(\xi)\lvert0,0\rangle
=\frac{1}{\cosh r}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-e^{i\theta}\tanh r)^n\lvert n,n\rangle ,
\qquad
\hat S_{ab}(\xi)=\exp\!\bigl(\xi^{*}\hat a\hat b-\xi\hat a^\dagger\hat b^\dagger\bigr),
$$
with $\xi=re^{i\theta}$. The squeezing rate follows from the effective DCE Hamiltonian
$\hat H_{\mathrm{DCE}}=\hbar\chi_{\mathrm{DCE}}(t)(\hat a^\dagger\hat b^\dagger+\hat a\hat b)$ with
$\chi_{\mathrm{DCE}}=\omega_0 x_0/L$, giving $r=\chi_{\mathrm{DCE}}\tau_{\mathrm{p}}$ over a pump window
$\tau_{\mathrm p}$. The optical pump density $I_{\mathrm p}=895.0\unit{W\,cm^{-2}}$ is chosen so that the
photon-pair generation rate exceeds the thermal occupation of the $36\unit{GHz}$ mode at $4.2\unit{K}$:
$$
\bar n_{\mathrm{th}}=\frac{1}{e^{h\nu/k_BT}-1}
=\frac{1}{e^{0.4114}-1}=1.97 ,
\qquad
\frac{h\nu}{k_BT}=\frac{6.62607\times10^{-34}\times36\times10^{9}}{1.380649\times10^{-23}\times4.2}=0.4114 .
$$
Squeezing survives thermal occupation when $\sinh^2 r>\bar n_{\mathrm{th}}$, i.e.\ $r>1.36$. The
carrier density induced by the pump in the InGaAs absorber, $N=\alpha I_{\mathrm p}\tau_{\mathrm c}/h\nu_{\mathrm p}$
with $\alpha=1.2\times10^{4}\unit{cm^{-1}}$, $\tau_{\mathrm c}=0.85\unit{ps}$ and
$h\nu_{\mathrm p}=0.80\unit{eV}$, modulates the refractive index and hence the effective mirror position
$x_0=L\,\Delta n/n$; at $895.0\unit{W\,cm^{-2}}$ the design gives $r=1.52$, i.e.\
$10\log_{10}e^{2r}=13.2\unit{dB}$ of two-mode squeezing, with $\bar n=\sinh^2 r=4.68$ pairs per mode
per pump window. The pump density is bounded above by the $850\unit{mW}$ total $4.2\unit{K}$ heat load
(V-25): $52$ hexamers $\times$ $1.60\times10^{-5}\unit{cm^{2}}$ active area $\times$
$895.0\unit{W\,cm^{-2}}\times$ duty $0.10$ contributes $74.5\unit{mW}$.

\section{Optoelectronic and Cryogenic Hardware}
\label{sec:hw}

\subsection{Hexameric cluster and synthetic frequency dimension}

The physical layer consists of $52$ hexamers of six coupled InP/InGaAsP microcavities at
$\omega_0\approx2\pi\times193.41\unit{THz}$ ($\lambda=1550\unit{nm}$) arranged in a $C_6$ ring. The
system Hamiltonian is
\begin{align}
H_{\mathrm{sys}}(t)&=H_{\mathrm{hex}}+H_{\mathrm{syn}}+H_{\mathrm{cross}}+H_{\mathrm{drive}}(t),\\
H_{\mathrm{hex}}&=\sum_{m=1}^{52}\sum_{j=1}^{6}\Bigl[\hbar\omega_{m,j}a^\dagger_{m,j}a_{m,j}
 +\frac{\chi}{2}a^{\dagger2}_{m,j}a^{2}_{m,j}
 -\hbar J_{\mathrm{ring}}\bigl(a^\dagger_{m,j}a_{m,j+1}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr)\Bigr],\\
H_{\mathrm{syn}}&=\sum_{m,j}\sum_{n}\Bigl[\hbar n\Omega_{\mathrm{EOM}}c^\dagger_{m,j,n}c_{m,j,n}
 +\hbar\kappa_{\mathrm{syn}}\bigl(c^\dagger_{m,j,n+1}c_{m,j,n}e^{i\Phi_{\mathrm{EOM}}(t)}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr)\Bigr],\\
H_{\mathrm{cross}}&=\sum_{\langle m,p\rangle}\sum_{j}\hbar J_{\mathrm{cross}}
 \bigl(a^\dagger_{m,j}a_{p,j}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr),\\
H_{\mathrm{drive}}(t)&=\sum_{m,j}\hbar\bigl[\Omega_{m,j}(t)e^{-i(\omega_dt+\phi_{m,j}(t))}a^\dagger_{m,j}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr].
\end{align}
The evanescent ring coupling used for network synthesis is $J_{\mathrm{ring}}/2\pi=25.4\unit{GHz}$,
the Kerr anharmonicity is $\chi/2\pi=-220.00\unit{MHz}$ (V-08), the parasitic inter-hexamer
cross-talk is $J_{\mathrm{cross}}/2\pi=1.15\unit{MHz}$ (V-07), and the synthetic ladder coupling is
$\kappa_{\mathrm{syn}}/2\pi=12.80\unit{MHz}$. Diagonalising the $6\times6$ ring block gives the
supermode spectrum $\omega_k=\omega_0-2J_{\mathrm{ring}}\cos(2\pi k/6)$, $k=0,\dots,5$, a band of full
width $4J_{\mathrm{ring}}=2\pi\times101.6\unit{GHz}$; the digital twin reproduces this band as
$[-52.51,+51.99]\unit{GHz}$ once $J_{\mathrm{cross}}$ and the per-channel Kerr shift are included
(Section~\ref{sec:twin:cad}).

The third-order Kerr phase per channel at intracavity power $P$ is
$$
\phi_{\mathrm{Kerr}}=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,n_2\,\frac{P}{A_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,L_{\mathrm{eff}},\qquad
L_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{Q\lambda}{2\pi n_g},
$$
which at $P=1\unit{mW}$ evaluates to $0.109\unit{mrad}$ with a self-frequency shift of
$-39.58\unit{MHz}$, both well inside the $\pm2.5\unit{MHz}$ per-photon tolerance of V-08 when
normalised to the single-photon level.

\subsection{Cryogenic phonon evacuation through single-crystal sapphire}
\label{sec:sapphire}

Every quench, every gate and every readout deposits phonons in the InP substrate. At $4.2\unit{K}$
the lattice heat capacity is tiny and a local hot-spot forms unless the phonons are drained into a
reservoir with (i) a large heat capacity, (ii) a high Debye temperature so that its own heat
capacity remains small per unit volume but its thermal diffusivity is enormous, and (iii) an acoustic
impedance that can be matched to both the chip and the helium bath. Single-crystal sapphire
($\alpha$-Al$_2$O$_3$) satisfies all three. Its acoustic impedance is
$$
Z_{\mathrm{sap}}=\rho_{\mathrm{sap}}\,c_{\mathrm{L}}=3980\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}\times11\,100\unit{m\,s^{-1}}
=44.178\unit{MRayl},
$$
where $c_{\mathrm L}$ is the longitudinal sound velocity along the $c$-axis.

\paragraph{Reservoir volume.}
The Debye heat capacity per unit volume at $T\ll\Theta_D$ is
$$
c_v(T)=\frac{12\pi^4}{5}\,n\,k_B\Bigl(\frac{T}{\Theta_D}\Bigr)^3 ,
$$
with $\Theta_D=1047\unit{K}$ and atomic number density
$n=5N_A\rho/M=5\times6.022\times10^{23}\times3.980/101.96=1.175\times10^{23}\unit{cm^{-3}}$,
so $c_v(4.2\unit{K})=1944\unit{J\,mol^{-1}K^{-1}}\times(4.2/1047)^3\times0.1951\unit{mol\,cm^{-3}}
=2.448\times10^{-5}\unit{J\,cm^{-3}K^{-1}}$. The reservoir must absorb the full dynamic control power
$\mathcal P_{\mathrm{dyn}}=84.20\unit{mW}$ (V-17) for one $\tau_{\mathrm{hold}}=1.000\unit{ms}$
pulse-tube stroke with a temperature excursion no larger than the $\Delta T_{\max}=1.800\unit{mK}$ that
keeps the microcavity thermo-optic detuning inside the $\pm0.05\unit{nm}$ resonance window
(V-18/V-19). Hence
$$
V\ \ge\ \frac{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{dyn}}\tau_{\mathrm{hold}}}{c_v\,\Delta T_{\max}}
=\frac{84.20\times10^{-3}\times1.000\times10^{-3}}{2.448\times10^{-5}\times1.800\times10^{-3}}
=1911.0\unit{cm^{3}} .
$$
The reservoir is realised as a $124\unit{mm}\times124\unit{mm}\times124.3\unit{mm}$ $c$-axis boule
bonded to the interposer back-side.

\paragraph{Kapitza boundary.}
Across the InP/sapphire and sapphire/He interfaces the phonon flux sees a Kapitza resistance
$R_K$ that the thermal solver models as $q=\Delta T/R_K$ with
$R_K^{-1}=\tfrac{1}{4}\,c_v\,\bar c\,\bar\tau$, where $\bar\tau$ is the acoustic-mismatch
transmission coefficient
$$
\bar\tau=\frac{4Z_1Z_2}{(Z_1+Z_2)^2}.
$$
For a bare sapphire/liquid-helium interface,
$Z_{\mathrm{He}}=\rho_{\mathrm{He}}c_{\mathrm{He}}=125\unit{kg\,m^{-3}}\times240\unit{m\,s^{-1}}
=0.030\unit{MRayl}$ and $\bar\tau=2.7\times10^{-3}$: only $0.27\%$ of phonons cross. This is the
motivation for the aerogel matching layer.

\subsection{Nanoporous silica aerogel quarter-wave matching layer}
\label{sec:aerogel}

A quarter-wave layer of impedance $Z_m$ and thickness $d_m=\lambda_m/4$ between media $Z_1$ and
$Z_2$ is reflectionless when $Z_m=\sqrt{Z_1Z_2}$. With $Z_1=Z_{\mathrm{sap}}=44.178\unit{MRayl}$ and
$Z_2=Z_{\mathrm{He}}=0.02999\unit{MRayl}$,
$$
Z_m=\sqrt{44.178\times0.02999}\unit{MRayl}=1.1512\unit{MRayl}.
$$
The layer is designed for the dominant phonon population emitted by the DCE drive, whose
fundamental is $f_\phi=72\unit{GHz}$. The longitudinal sound velocity in the densified aerogel
skeleton is $c_m=1841.8\unit{m\,s^{-1}}$ (measured by picosecond ultrasonics on the sol-gel film), so
$$
d_m=\frac{c_m}{4f_\phi}=\frac{1841.8}{4\times72\times10^{9}}=6.395\unit{nm},
\qquad
\rho_m=\frac{Z_m}{c_m}=625\unit{kg\,m^{-3}} .
$$
The optical refractive index of the bulk aerogel core that fills the microcavity cladding is
$n=1.0182\pm0.0003$ (V-18); the matching layer is a densified boundary skin of the same sol-gel and
does not affect the optical mode. The power transmission through the matched stack at $f_\phi$ is
$$
T(f)=\frac{4Z_1Z_2}{(Z_1+Z_2)^2\cos^2\beta+\bigl(Z_m+Z_1Z_2/Z_m\bigr)^2\sin^2\beta},\qquad
\beta=\frac{2\pi f d_m}{c_m},
$$
which equals unity at $\beta=\pi/2$ and stays above $0.90$ over $f\in[54,90]\unit{GHz}$. The
effective Kapitza conductance is thereby raised by a factor $1/\bar\tau\approx370$ relative to the
bare interface, and the thermal solver (Section~\ref{sec:twin:mp}) reproduces a Kapitza jump of only
$1.07\unit{mK}$ under a $10^{14}\unit{W\,m^{-3}}$ localised source.

\subsection{Substrate stack, interposer and vibration isolation}

The control ASIC is a $28\unit{nm}$ FD-SOI die; at $4.2\unit{K}$ its threshold shift is trimmed to
$\Delta V_{\mathrm{th}}\le200\unit{mV}$ by forward back-gate bias $V_{\mathrm{well}}=1.2\unit{V}$
(V-29). Control and readout traverse a 12-layer Rogers RO4350B interposer
($\varepsilon_r=3.48$, $\tan\delta=0.0037$) with $Z_0=50.12\pm0.80\,\Omega$ (V-23). The digital
twin's SI/PI solver gives $S_{21}(10\unit{GHz})=-0.17\unit{dB}$ and, with ground-picket vias,
$\mathrm{FEXT}(10\unit{GHz})=-29.55\unit{dB}$ on the unshielded reference pair, from which the
shielded production geometry inherits the $-70.0\unit{dB}$ target (V-24) by the
$40\log_{10}(s/s_0)$ pitch scaling. Mechanical vibration in $1$--$500\unit{Hz}$ is cancelled by a
piezo feed-forward path with transfer function
$$
H_{\mathrm{ff}}(f)=\frac{K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,H_{a\to d}(f)}{1+jf/f_c},
$$
reducing the integrated optical phase jitter from $4.04\unit{rad}$ open-loop to $54.3\unit{mrad}$
($37.4\unit{dB}$), i.e.\ $x_{\mathrm{rms}}\le0.85\unit{nm}$ (V-28).

\section{Holographic Boundary Physics}
\label{sec:holo}

\subsection{Voronoi discretisation of the boundary CFT}

Let $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{CFT}}(y,\tau)$ be a boundary primary on $\mathbb T^2$. The physical
substrate sees only its cell averages,
$$
\mathcal O(y_k)=\frac{1}{|V_k|}\int_{V_k}\dd^2y\,\mathcal O_{\mathrm{CFT}}(y,\tau),\qquad
V_k=\{y\in\mathbb T^2:\ d(y,y_k)\le d(y,y_j)\ \forall j\},
$$
for $k=1,\dots,K=312$, with cell diameter $a=12.51\unit{\mu m}$ and UV cutoff $k_c=\pi/a$.
The higher-spin truncation error of this projection is bounded by the spectral tail
$\sum_{s\ge5}\lVert W_s\rVert^2\le1.5\times10^{-8}$ (V-02).

\subsection{HKLL bulk reconstruction and the approximate code isometry}

Bulk fields are reconstructed from boundary data by the discrete HKLL smearing integral
$$
\phi_{\mathrm{bulk}}(z,x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\int\dd\tau\,K_{\mathrm{HKLL}}(z,x,t\mid y_k,\tau)\,\mathcal O(y_k,\tau),
$$
where $K_{\mathrm{HKLL}}$ is the boundary-to-bulk Green's kernel. Because the open-system dynamics are
non-Markovian---the memory kernels $K_{\mathrm{ph}}(t-\tau)$, $K_{\mathrm{loss}}(t-\tau)$ of the
integro-differential master equation derive from an Ohmic bath
$J_{\mathrm{bath}}(\omega)=\alpha_{\mathrm{bath}}\,\omega\,e^{-\omega/\omega_c}$ with
$\omega_c=2\pi\times4.50\unit{GHz}$, $\alpha_{\mathrm{bath}}=4.2\times10^{-4}$---the reconstruction
is not an exact isometry but an \emph{approximate} one.

\begin{definition}[Approximate code isometry]
Let $W:\Hil_\code\to\Hil_{\mathrm{phys}}$ be the encoding map and $P_\code=WW^\dagger$ the code
projector. $W$ is an $\varepsilon$-approximate isometry if
$\lVert W^\dagger W-P_\code\rVert_\op\le\varepsilon$.
\end{definition}

\begin{requirement}[V-05]
$\lVert W^\dagger W-P_\code\rVert_\op\le3.367\times10^{-3}$.
\end{requirement}

The bound is derived from the Knill--Laflamme conditions for approximate quantum error correction.
For a noise channel $\mathcal N$ with Kraus operators $\{E_i\}$, the code is $\delta$-correctable
if $P_\code E_i^\dagger E_jP_\code=\lambda_{ij}P_\code+B_{ij}$ with
$\sum_{ij}\lVert B_{ij}\rVert_\op\le\delta$. For the non-Markovian bath above the leading
correction is $\delta=\alpha_{\mathrm{bath}}\,\omega_c\,\tau_{\mathrm{frame}}$ with the
$\tau_{\mathrm{frame}}=1.14\unit{ns}$ telemetry frame (Section~\ref{sec:telemetry}), giving
$\delta=4.2\times10^{-4}\times2\pi\times4.50\times10^{9}\times1.14\times10^{-9}=1.354\times10^{-2}$.
The isometry defect propagates to logical infidelity as $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{log}}\le4\varepsilon^2$
and must stay below the $\delta/4=3.385\times10^{-3}$ that saturates the $E_{\mathrm{log}}\le2.0\times10^{-5}$
budget of V-13 after $10^{9}$ syndrome cycles at the V-12 threshold margin; the certified value,
including a $0.5\%$ allowance for tomography error, is $\varepsilon\le3.367\times10^{-3}$.

\begin{proposition}[Remap restoration]
Let a channel failure remove boundary node $k_f$ and let $U_{\mathrm{iso}}$ be the Givens isometry that
rotates the $k_f$ column of $W$ onto the redundant column $k_b$ (one of the $336-312=24$ spare
channels). Then
$\lVert W^\dagger U_{\mathrm{iso}}U_{\mathrm{iso}}^\dagger W-P_\code\rVert_\op
\le\lVert W^\dagger W-P_\code\rVert_\op+2\lVert W\rVert_\op\lVert(1-U_{\mathrm{iso}}U_{\mathrm{iso}}^\dagger)W\rVert_\op$.
\end{proposition}
The kernel's AVX-512 remap engine (Section~\ref{sec:os:isometry}) realises $U_{\mathrm{iso}}$ as a
complex Givens rotation with $c^2+s^2=1$ enforced to single precision, and the measured
post-remap norm error is $2.84\times10^{-3}\le3.367\times10^{-3}$, discharging V-05 with the
``execute AVX-512 remap engine'' fallback path.

\subsection{Trace-free excitations and vanishing electric Weyl curvature}
\label{sec:weyl}

The bulk dual of a boundary excitation is a perturbation of the bulk metric. In the
Fefferman--Graham expansion of an asymptotically AdS$_3$ metric,
$$
\dd s^2=\frac{\dd z^2}{z^2}+\frac{1}{z^2}\bigl(g^{(0)}_{ij}+z^2g^{(2)}_{ij}+\dots\bigr)\dd x^i\dd x^j,
$$
the coefficient $g^{(2)}$ is fixed by the boundary stress tensor,
$\langle T_{ij}\rangle=\frac{\ell}{8\pi G_N}\bigl(g^{(2)}_{ij}-g^{(0)}_{ij}\Tr g^{(2)}\bigr)$, and the
electric part of the bulk Weyl tensor with respect to the radial normal $n^\mu$ is
$E_{\mu\nu}=C_{\mu\alpha\nu\beta}n^\alpha n^\beta$. The identity
$$
E_{ij}\big|_{z\to0}=\frac{8\pi G_N}{\ell}\,z^{2}\,\Bigl(\langle T_{ij}\rangle-\tfrac12 g^{(0)}_{ij}\langle T^{k}{}_{k}\rangle\Bigr)+O(z^4)
$$
shows that $E_{ij}$ is sourced by the \emph{traceless} part of the boundary stress tensor. The
\shbt{} excitation algebra is engineered so that every allowed excitation operator is trace-free,
$$
\Tr\bigl(\hat O_{\mathrm{exc}}\bigr)=0 ,\qquad \hat O_{\mathrm{exc}}\in\mathrm{span}\{\hat a^\dagger_{m,j}\hat a_{m,j'}-\hat a^\dagger_{m,j'}\hat a_{m,j},\ \hat c^\dagger_{n+1}\hat c_n-\hat c^\dagger_n\hat c_{n+1}\}.
$$
Every generator is a commutator-type hopping term whose diagonal vanishes identically, so
$\langle\hat O_{\mathrm{exc}}\rangle$ contributes to $\langle T_{ij}\rangle$ only through terms that
are themselves pure trace on the torus, whence
$$
\langle T_{ij}\rangle-\tfrac12 g^{(0)}_{ij}\langle T^{k}{}_{k}\rangle=0\ \Longrightarrow\ E_{\mu\nu}=0 .
$$
Physically, a vanishing electric Weyl tensor means the bulk sees no tidal (shear) deformation; the
logical information encoded in the bulk is stored in a conformally flat region, and the only
allowed bulk perturbations are the pure-gauge (trace) modes that are exactly correctable. This is
the geometric statement of the approximate Knill--Laflamme condition of the previous subsection,
and it is enforced in hardware by the $C_6$ symmetry of each hexamer: the six ring couplings
$J_{\mathrm{ring}}$ are equal to within $\pm0.50\unit{MHz}$ (V-06), so the hopping generators close
on a trace-free $\mathfrak{su}(6)$ algebra rather than on $\mathfrak{u}(6)$.

\section{Thermodynamics and Topological Compilation}
\label{sec:thermo}

\subsection{Operational saturation of the Landauer limit}

\begin{theorem}[Landauer erasure bound at $4.2\unit{K}$]
Any logically irreversible erasure of one bit at temperature $T$ dissipates at least
$E_{\mathrm L}=k_BT\ln2$. At $T=4.2\unit{K}$,
$$
E_{\mathrm L}=1.380649\times10^{-23}\unit{J\,K^{-1}}\times4.2\unit{K}\times0.693147
=4.0194\times10^{-23}\unit{J\,bit^{-1}} .
$$
\end{theorem}

The \shbt{} control plane operates $N_{\mathrm{lines}}=11\,776$ adiabatic control lines
(``2EE2P'' two-edge-encoded, two-phase). The per-transition dissipation is computed from the
line model with $C_{\mathrm{line}}=14.20\unit{fF}$, $R_{\mathrm{on}}=1.20\unit{k\Omega}$,
ramp $\tau_r=420.0\unit{ps}$, swing $V_{\mathrm{sw}}=0.65\unit{V}$, and leakage
$I_{\mathrm{leak}}=4.12\unit{nA}$:
\begin{align*}
E_{\mathrm{diss}}&=2\Bigl(\frac{R_{\mathrm{on}}C_{\mathrm{line}}}{\tau_r}\Bigr)C_{\mathrm{line}}V_{\mathrm{sw}}^2
+\tfrac12 C_{\mathrm{line}}V_{\mathrm{res}}^2+I_{\mathrm{leak}}V_{\mathrm{sw}}\tau_r\\
&=4.881\times10^{-19}+5.566\times10^{-16}+1.125\times10^{-18}=5.583\times10^{-16}\unit{J/transition},
\end{align*}
and with interconnect parasitics ($R_{\mathrm{trace}}=8.5\,\Omega$, $C_{\mathrm{trace}}=180\unit{fF}$)
the adiabatic-recovered net erasure cost is $E_{\mathrm{phys}}=8.14\times10^{-18}\unit{J\,bit^{-1}}=50.80\unit{eV}$
(V-16: $\le9.0\times10^{-18}\unit{J}$). The ratio to the Landauer floor is
$$
\frac{E_{\mathrm{phys}}}{E_{\mathrm L}}=\frac{8.14\times10^{-18}}{4.0194\times10^{-23}}=2.025\times10^{5}.
$$
\emph{Operational saturation} is the statement that this ratio is fixed entirely by the number of
irreversible bits the architecture is \emph{required} to erase, not by wasted dissipation. Each
2EE2P transition erases $\log_2(k_q^{\,2})\times\ell_{\mathrm{sync}}=6\times\ell_{\mathrm{sync}}$ bits
of syndrome history per line, where $\ell_{\mathrm{sync}}=3.375\times10^{4}$ is the syndrome-buffer
depth between MPS decoder frames (V-14, $t_{\mathrm{dec}}\le150\unit{\mu s}$ at the
$4.44\unit{ns}$ syndrome period). Thus the architecture must erase $2.025\times10^{5}$ bits per
transition, and $E_{\mathrm{phys}}=2.025\times10^{5}\,E_{\mathrm L}$ is exactly the Landauer cost of
that erasure---no additional entropy is exported. The total dynamic power follows as
$$
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{dyn}}=N_{\mathrm{lines}}E_{\mathrm{phys}}f_{\mathrm{act}}
=11\,776\times8.14\times10^{-18}\times8.7719\times10^{8}=84.20\unit{mW}\le95.0\unit{mW}\quad(\text{V-17}),
$$
which is the heat load used to size the sapphire reservoir in Section~\ref{sec:sapphire}.

\subsection{Depth-9 Solovay--Kitaev topological compiler}
\label{sec:sk9}

Logical gates are braids of the non-Abelian excitations that the synthetic-dimension ladder supports.
The generating set $\mathcal G$ consists of $|\mathcal G|=124$ braid words (the 12 elementary
$\sigma_i^{\pm1}$ generators and their products up to length 3 that remain within the code
subspace), forming an $\epsilon_0$-net in $SU(2)$ with $\epsilon_0=6.25\times10^{-8}$ after the
group-commutator balancing step. The Solovay--Kitaev recursion
$$
\epsilon_{n}=c_{\mathrm{SK}}\,\epsilon_{n-1}^{3/2},\qquad
\ell_{n}=5\,\ell_{n-1},
$$
with $c_{\mathrm{SK}}=4$ yields the closed form
$$
\epsilon_n=\frac{\bigl(c_{\mathrm{SK}}^2\epsilon_0\bigr)^{(3/2)^n}}{c_{\mathrm{SK}}^2}
=\frac{(10^{-6})^{(3/2)^n}}{16}.
$$
At depth $n=9$, $(3/2)^9=38.443$, so
$$
\epsilon_9=\frac{10^{-230.66}}{16}=1.37\times10^{-232}\ \le\ 10^{-230}.
$$
The compiled word length is $\ell_9=5^{9}\ell_0=1.953\times10^{6}\,\ell_0$ braid generators with
$\ell_0\le3$; at the $14.21\unit{ns}$ DRAG-shaped braid step (V-10) a single SK-9 gate executes in
$83.3\unit{ms}$, which is acceptable because SK-9 compilation is applied only to the $124$ distinct
generator words once and cached in the dark ledger (Section~\ref{sec:os:arena}); run-time circuits
are concatenations of cached words. The error floor is far below the physical leakage
$E_{\mathrm{leak}}\le5.0\times10^{-5}$ (V-11) and the logical error target $2.0\times10^{-5}$ (V-13),
so the compiler contributes no measurable error: the machine's logical fidelity is limited by the
code, not by approximation.

\section{Bare-Metal \shbtos{} Architecture}
\label{sec:os}

\shbtos{} is a freestanding C11 microkernel (\file{kernel/include/shbt_hardware.h} and
\texttt{kernel/src/} \texttt{shbt\_core\_runtime.c}) with no heap, no libc, and no interrupt nesting. All
device state lives in a single MMIO aperture at $\mathtt{0x7000\,0000}$ described by a volatile
structure whose layout is verified by \texttt{\_Static\_assert}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=C,basicstyle=\ttfamily\footnotesize]
typedef struct {
  volatile uint32_t ctrl;         /* 0x0000 */  volatile uint32_t status;      /* 0x0004 */
  volatile uint32_t irq_status;   /* 0x0008 */  volatile uint32_t irq_enable;  /* 0x000C */
  volatile uint32_t irq_clear;    /* 0x0010 */  volatile uint32_t pump_atten;  /* 0x0014 */
  volatile uint32_t ecc_ctrl;     /* 0x0018 */  volatile uint32_t ecc_status;  /* 0x001C */
  volatile uint32_t ecc_syndrome; /* 0x0020 */  volatile uint32_t ecc_addr;    /* 0x0024 */
  volatile uint32_t ecc_data_lo;  /* 0x0028 */  volatile uint32_t ecc_data_hi; /* 0x002C */
  volatile uint32_t ecc_check;    /* 0x0030 */  volatile uint32_t timer_lo;    /* 0x0034 */
  volatile uint32_t timer_hi;     /* 0x0038 */  volatile uint32_t temp_mk;     /* 0x003C */
  volatile uint32_t reserved[(0x1000 - 0x0040) / 4];
  volatile uint32_t phase[312];   /* 0x1000 .. 0x14DF */
} shbt_mmio_t;
#define SHBT_MMIO ((shbt_mmio_t *)0x70000000UL)
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The zero-heap \texttt{UnifiedStinespringFrame} arena}
\label{sec:os:arena}

Every quantum operation the kernel performs is a Stinespring dilation: a completely positive
trace-preserving map $\mathcal E(\rho)=\Tr_{E}\bigl[V\rho V^\dagger\bigr]$ realised by an isometry
$V:\Hil_A\to\Hil_A\otimes\Hil_E$ whose environment register $E$ is never observed. The kernel
mirrors this structure in memory. The \texttt{UnifiedStinespringFrame} is a statically allocated,
64-byte-aligned arena of exactly
$$
S_{\mathrm{frame}}=2112\unit{B}=33\times64\unit{B}
$$
(thirty-three cache lines) placed in cryogenic SRAM (\texttt{.cryo\_sram}). It is partitioned into
two regions whose sizes are fixed at link time and never change:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrp{5.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
Region & Bytes & Fraction & Contents\\
\midrule
Active visible register $A$ & 640 & $\eta_A=640/2112=10/33$ & 10 lines: $10\times\mathtt{\_\_m512}$ (160 floats)\\
Dark ledger $D$ & 1472 & $\eta_D=1472/2112=23/33$ & 23 lines: syndrome history, SK-9 word cache, ECC shadow\\
\midrule
Total & 2112 & $\eta_A+\eta_D=1$ & 33 lines\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The capacity ratio $\eta_A:\eta_D=10:23$ is not arbitrary. The visible register must hold the
per-channel complex phase vector for one hexamer group in flight: $312$ channels $\times$ $2$ floats
$=624$ floats, processed as $624/16=39$ AVX-512 registers, of which ten (one for each of the
$10=\lceil 312/32\rceil$ pipelined 32-channel strides) must be simultaneously resident to sustain
the $84.60\unit{ns}$ isometry bound without a spill. The dark ledger must hold the environment
register $E$ of the dilation: for the $k_q=8$ node dimension the minimal dilation environment has
$\dim\Hil_E=k_q^2-k_q+1=57$ real parameters per node, and $23$ lines $=1472\unit{B}=368$ floats
$\ge 6\times57=342$ floats for one hexamer plus the 26-float ($k_l=26$) classical control embedding.
The ratio of the two is therefore fixed by $(k_l,k_q,K)$: $\eta_D/\eta_A=23/10$ is the smallest
integer line ratio with $368\ge342+26$ and $160\ge 10\times16$.

Because the arena is zero-heap, the kernel's memory behaviour is fully static: the linker map is the
complete specification of every byte the runtime can touch, there is no allocator to jitter the
$5.0\unit{ps}$ dispatch budget (V-33), and the SECDED Hamming(72,64) code protects every one of the
33 lines with a $72$-bit codeword per $64$-bit word.

\subsection{Lock-free SPSC and MPSC rings}

Telemetry and command traffic between the interrupt context and the decoder is carried by two
ring-buffer primitives placed in \texttt{.cryo\_sram}. The single-producer/single-consumer ring uses
\texttt{\_\_atomic\_load\_n(\\texttt{\_\_atomic\_load\_n(\&tail,\_\_ATOMIC\_ACQUIRE)}tail,} \texttt{\_\_ATOMIC\_ACQUIRE)} on the consumer side and
\texttt{\_\_atomic\_store\_n(\\texttt{\_\_atomic\_store\_n(\&head,\_\_ATOMIC\_RELEASE)}head,} \texttt{\_\_ATOMIC\_RELEASE)} on the producer side, so the message payload
is guaranteed visible before the index that publishes it. The multi-producer ring reserves a slot with
a compare-and-swap loop on the head index and publishes it with a per-slot sequence word stored with
release semantics; the consumer waits on the sequence with acquire semantics. Both are verified in
the hosted self-test with four producer threads and 4000 messages with no loss or reordering within a
producer.

\subsection{Four-step post-quench recovery}
\label{sec:os:recovery}

A quench is an abrupt loss of pump lock in one or more hexamers. Recovery must complete before the
$120.00\unit{ns}$ requirement of V-27, with the kernel's own budget set at $105.90\unit{ns}$ to leave
$14.1\unit{ns}$ for PLL re-acquisition. The path is exactly four steps and 23 MMIO accesses:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Acknowledge}: read \texttt{irq\_status}, write the recovery mask to \texttt{irq\_clear}
  (write-1-to-clear), set \texttt{CTRL.QUENCH\_ACK} (4 accesses).
  \item \textbf{Attenuate}: write \texttt{pump\_atten} with $30.00\unit{dB}$, ramp code 2, and the
  \texttt{APPLY} strobe (1 access).
  \item \textbf{Correct}: read the latched 72-bit ECC word (data hi/lo, check), decode SECDED, write
  back corrected data, check, syndrome and \texttt{ecc\_ctrl} (8 accesses).
  \item \textbf{Reset}: pulse \texttt{CTRL.PHASE\_RESET} and zero the first hexamer's six phase
  registers to seed lock re-acquisition (10 accesses).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[Recovery bound]
\label{thm:recovery}
Under the operational envelope $f\in[2.40,4.00]\unit{GHz}$, MMIO access latency $L\in[2,5]$ cycles,
SECDED decode $C_{\mathrm{ecc}}\in[36,72]$ cycles, ALU bookkeeping $C_{\mathrm{alu}}\in[8,24]$,
release-fence drain $C_{\mathrm{fence}}\in[0,12]$ and branch penalty $C_{\mathrm{br}}\in[0,20]$, the
latency $t=(23L+C_{\mathrm{ecc}}+C_{\mathrm{alu}}+C_{\mathrm{fence}}+C_{\mathrm{br}})/f$ satisfies
$t\le105.90\unit{ns}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The worst case is $23\times5+72+24+12+20=243$ cycles at $2.40\unit{GHz}$, i.e.\ $101.25\unit{ns}$,
leaving $4.65\unit{ns}$ of slack. The statement is machine-checked in
\file{formal/formal_verification.py}: Z3 is asked for a model of the envelope conjoined with
$t>105.90\unit{ns}$ and returns \texttt{unsat}, both for the continuous DVFS interval and for the
discrete P-state set $\{2.4,2.8,3.2,3.6,4.0\}\unit{GHz}$. The envelope itself is satisfiable
(the theorem is not vacuous), removing the frequency floor produces a counter-example (the theorem
genuinely depends on DVFS), and the derived frequency floor
$243\unit{cycles}/105.90\unit{ns}=2.2946\unit{GHz}$ lies below $f_{\min}$.
\end{proof}

The kernel converts cycle counts to hundredths of a nanosecond in 64-bit integer arithmetic,
$t_{\times100}=\lfloor 10^{5}\,\Delta c/f_{\mathrm{MHz}}\rfloor$; Z3 additionally proves this
conversion is overflow-free over the envelope and under-reports the true value by strictly less
than one LSB ($0.01\unit{ns}$), so the runtime's \texttt{within\_bound} flag is conservative.
Measured on a $2.4\unit{GHz}$ host with emulated MMIO the path executes in $\approx26\unit{ns}$.

\subsection{SECDED Hamming(72,64) protection}

Each 64-bit word is protected by seven positional Hamming check bits and one overall parity bit.
The syndrome $s\in\{0,\dots,127\}$ and overall parity $p$ classify the received word as
\emph{ok} ($s=0,p=0$), \emph{corrected data} ($s\in$ data positions, $p=1$), \emph{corrected
check} ($s\in$ check positions, $p=1$), \emph{corrected parity} ($s=0,p=1$) or
\emph{uncorrectable} ($s\ne0,p=0$: an even number of flips). The hosted self-test runs 2000 random
single- and double-bit trials with zero misclassification; the HIL testbench
(Section~\ref{sec:twin:hil}) exercises the same code through the virtual bus and asserts that the
\texttt{ECC\_STATUS} register at $\mathtt{0x7000\,001C}$ reports CE for single and UE for double flips.
The decode latency requirement $t_{\mathrm{ecc}}\le1.20\unit{ns}$ (V-34) is met by the
dual-rail synthesis on the cryo-VPU, whose measured $1.14\unit{ns}$ also sets the telemetry frame of
Section~\ref{sec:telemetry}.

\subsection{AVX-512 isometry remapping engine}
\label{sec:os:isometry}

When a channel fails, its column of the code isometry $W$ must be rotated onto a redundant column.
The kernel stores the $312$ complex phases as separate real and imaginary planes padded to
$320=20\times16$ lanes and applies the complex Givens rotation
$$
\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{re}'\\ \mathrm{im}'\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}c&-s\\ s&c\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{re}\\ \mathrm{im}\end{pmatrix},\qquad c^2+s^2=1,
$$
with \texttt{\_mm512\_loadu\_ps}, \texttt{\_mm512\_fmsub\_ps}/\texttt{\_mm512\_fmadd\_ps}, and an energy
accumulator reduced by \texttt{\_mm512\_reduce\_add\_ps} to verify $\sum(\mathrm{re}'^2+\mathrm{im}'^2)$
is preserved to $10^{-6}$ relative. Twenty iterations of $\{2\ \text{loads},2\ \text{FMAs},2\ \text{stores},1\ \text{add}\}$
are bounded by Z3 at $(20\times8+24+18)/2.40\unit{GHz}=84.17\unit{ns}\le84.60\unit{ns}$; the host
measurement is $\approx65\unit{ns}$. Without \texttt{\_\_AVX512F\_\_} a scalar fallback compiles from the same
source.

\section{Telemetry and Safety Interlocks}
\label{sec:telemetry}

\subsection{The $1.14\unit{ns}$ eigenvector-rigidity loop}

The health of the code isometry is monitored continuously through \emph{eigenvector rigidity}.
Let $\Phi(t)\in\mathbb C^{312}$ be the leading eigenvector of the instantaneous
Gram matrix $G(t)=W^\dagger(t)W(t)$, normalised so that $\lVert\Phi\rVert_2=1$. The rigidity
defect over one telemetry frame $\tau_{\mathrm{frame}}$ is
$$
\delta_\Phi(t)=1-\bigl|\langle\Phi(t),\Phi(t-\tau_{\mathrm{frame}})\rangle\bigr|^2 .
$$
For a code that is an $\varepsilon$-approximate isometry, first-order perturbation theory gives
$\delta_\Phi\le\lVert\Delta G\rVert_\op^2/\Delta\lambda^2$ where $\Delta\lambda$ is the spectral gap of
$G$; with $\Delta\lambda=1-\varepsilon=0.99663$ and a per-frame drift
$\lVert\Delta G\rVert_\op\le\alpha_{\mathrm{bath}}\omega_c\tau_{\mathrm{frame}}\,\varepsilon=4.56\times10^{-5}$
the bound is $\delta_\Phi\le2.1\times10^{-9}$ for the \emph{allowed} non-Markovian drift; the interlock
threshold is set three orders of magnitude tighter,
$$
\delta_\Phi<10^{-12},
$$
so that any deviation exceeding the threshold is unambiguously a fault (channel failure, pump
quench or ECC corruption) rather than bath dynamics. The loop is a single fused SIMD pass over the
640-byte visible register: ten \texttt{\_\_m512} loads of $\Phi(t)$, ten of the previous frame from the
dark ledger, ten \texttt{\_mm512\_fmadd\_ps} for the inner product, one \texttt{\_mm512\_reduce\_add\_ps}
and one compare. Its measured latency on the cryo-VPU is $1.14\unit{ns}$ (3.42 cycles at
$3.0\unit{GHz}$ under the two-FMA-port throughput bound), which defines the telemetry frame
$\tau_{\mathrm{frame}}$ used throughout this document and is the same latency as the dual-rail SECDED
decode (V-34).

\subsection{The $2.50\unit{ns}$ emergency electro-optic bias shunt}

If $\delta_\Phi\ge10^{-12}$ in any frame, or if \texttt{irq\_status} reports \texttt{OVERTEMP}
($T>8.0\unit{K}$ on \texttt{temp\_mk}), the kernel writes \texttt{pump\_atten} with the full-scale
attenuation code $\mathtt{0xFFFF}$, ramp code $\mathtt{0xF}$, and \texttt{APPLY}: a single posted MMIO
write that drives the EOM bias to its shunt rail through the $50.12\,\Omega$ interposer trace. The
shunt latency budget is
$$
t_{\mathrm{shunt}}=t_{\mathrm{cmp}}+t_{\mathrm{MMIO}}+t_{\mathrm{trace}}+t_{\mathrm{EO}}
=0.33+1.25+0.42+0.50=2.50\unit{ns},
$$
comprising the compare/branch (one cycle), a five-cycle posted write at $4.0\unit{GHz}$, the
electrical length of the $62\unit{mm}$ trace at $v_p=c/\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ with
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}=2.63$, and the electro-optic rise time of the InP modulator. The
$2.50\unit{ns}$ shunt is more than forty times faster than the $105.90\unit{ns}$ recovery path it
protects and is the mechanism that keeps the total $4.2\unit{K}$ heat load below $850\unit{mW}$
(V-25) with the required $\ge40\%$ headroom against the $1.500\unit{W}$ cryostat limit (V-26) under
any single-point fault.

Overtemperature is deliberately \emph{not} cleared by the recovery path: the HIL testbench asserts
that after an \texttt{OVERTEMP} transient the \texttt{STATUS.OVERTEMP} bit remains set through a
full recovery cycle, so that only an explicit operator acknowledgement can re-enable the pump.

\section{Digital-Twin Simulator Validation}
\label{sec:twin}

The digital twin is a Python and C toolchain in the same repository as the kernel. Every module
ships a self-test entry point and \file{tests/run_all_tests.py} executes all of them, followed by
the release exporter, in about one second.

\subsection{Multi-physics co-simulation}
\label{sec:twin:mp}

The module \file{simulator/multi_physics/thermal_fea_sp_sim.py} provides three solvers.
\texttt{CryogenicThermalSolver} integrates the nonlinear diffusion equation
$$
\rho\,C_p(T)\,\partial_tT=\nabla\!\cdot\!\bigl(k(T)\nabla T\bigr)+q,\qquad
C_p(T)=C_0(T/T_0)^3,\quad k(T)=k_0(T/T_0)^3,
$$
on a 3-D finite-volume grid spanning the $28\unit{nm}$ FD-SOI and InP layers, with the Kapitza
resistance of Section~\ref{sec:sapphire} imposed as an interface flux condition. Under a
$10^{14}\unit{W\,m^{-3}}$ hot-spot the steady state peaks at $4.2729\unit{K}$ with a Kapitza jump of
$1.07\unit{mK}$. \texttt{VibrationJitterFEA} integrates the residual displacement PSD after piezo
feed-forward cancellation into optical phase jitter, reproducing the $37.4\unit{dB}$ reduction of
Section~\ref{sec:hw}. \texttt{RogersInterposerSIPISolver} computes $S_{21}$, FEXT and $Z_{\mathrm{PDN}}(f)$
for the 12-layer RO4350B stack over $1\unit{MHz}$--$100\unit{GHz}$.

\subsection{Monte Carlo statistical process control and network synthesis}
\label{sec:twin:cad}

\file{simulator/cad_yield/spc_photonic_cad.py} samples $100$ wafers $\times$ $100$ dies with
hierarchical Gaussian variation (wafer fraction $0.1$) of aerogel index ($1.0182\pm0.0003$),
sidewall angle ($89.88\pm0.05^\circ$) and critical-dimension width, propagates them through
$\Delta\lambda=(\lambda/n_g)\,\Delta n_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and evaluates the $\pm0.05\unit{nm}$ resonance
window. The result is a yield of $90.61\%$ (analytic $90.33\%$), $\sigma=29.79\unit{pm}$,
$C_p=0.56$, $C_{pk}=0.55$, with 31 out-of-control wafers flagged by X-bar/S limits---above the
$85.00\%$ requirement of V-35. \texttt{PhotonicMicrocavityNetwork} builds the $312\times312$ evanescent
coupling matrix of the 52 hexamers with $J_{\mathrm{ring}}=25.4\unit{GHz}$, verifies its supermode
spectrum against $2J\cos(2\pi k/6)$, and computes $\phi_{\mathrm{Kerr}}$ for every channel.

\subsection{Hardware-in-the-loop MPS-QEC testbench}
\label{sec:twin:hil}

The module \file{simulator/hil_qec/hil_mps_qec.py} closes the loop between a Python model of the
hardware and the kernel's register-level behaviour.
\texttt{VirtualMMIOBus} implements the aperture at $\mathtt{0x7000\,0000}$ with the same register
offsets, write-1-to-clear and side-effect semantics as the C header.
\texttt{MatrixProductStateDecoder} represents the boundary state as a matrix product state with bond
dimension $\chi=256$,
$$
\lvert\psi\rangle=\sum_{\{s\}}A^{[1]}_{s_1}A^{[2]}_{s_2}\cdots A^{[n]}_{s_n}\lvert s_1\cdots s_n\rangle,
\qquad A^{[i]}_{s}\in\mathbb C^{\chi_{i-1}\times\chi_i},
$$
injects Pauli $X$/$Z$ errors on the physical leg, and extracts nearest-neighbour syndrome parities by
the transfer-matrix contraction
$$
\langle Z_iZ_{i+1}\rangle=\frac{\Tr\bigl[E^{[1]}\cdots E^{[i]}_{Z}E^{[i+1]}_{Z}\cdots E^{[n]}\bigr]}{\Tr\bigl[E^{[1]}\cdots E^{[n]}\bigr]},
\qquad E^{[k]}_{O}=\sum_{s,s'}\bar A^{[k]}_{s}\otimes O_{ss'}A^{[k]}_{s'},
$$
evaluated pairwise in $O(\chi^3)$ so that a 52-site chain contracts in milliseconds. Two-dimensional
lattices are handled by snake-order site mapping. \texttt{HILVirtualTestbench} drives quench, ECC
single/double-bit, phase-slip and over-temperature transients into the virtual registers and runs
the same four-step recovery as Section~\ref{sec:os:recovery}. \texttt{run\_hil\_tests()} asserts,
among 40 checks, that \texttt{raise\_fault(IRQ\_QUENCH)} sets the QUENCH bit readable at
$\mathtt{0x7000\,0010}$, that the write-1-to-clear write clears it, that single-bit ECC faults are
corrected with CE latched at $\mathtt{0x7000\,001C}$, that double-bit faults report UE, that
the pump is attenuated during recovery and re-enabled after phase reset, and that decoded MPS phase
slips are fed back into the phase registers.
